“The breakthrough starts with microscopy. Researchers, including teams at Google, help reconstruct neurons and their connections. They cut the fly's brain into extremely thin layers, scanned them, then traced individual neurons across layers and located their synapses, building a 3D map of the brain's wiring. Other teams turn those maps into mathematical brain models and connect them to virtual bodies. But the neural dynamics, basically the connection between the endocrine system, neurotransmitter effects, and physiology, still need to be modeled.”

“These are recordings from our simulator: same fly, same brain, same arena. We change one setting at a time.

Left: we stimulate reward dopamine. The brain's decision swings to approach, and the fly works its way around the danger to the food.

Middle: we stimulate punishment dopamine instead. The approach signal collapses. The fly hesitates and walks away.

Right: we switch the danger smell off. Nothing warns the fly, and it walks straight under the newspaper.

In the full physics simulation, eight runs per setting, the pattern holds: the fly reaches the food 3 times out of 8 at baseline, 8 out of 8 with reward dopamine stimulated, and 1 out of 8 with punishment dopamine stimulated. Small samples, but a clear signal we can test.”

“If we recreate parts of an animal's brain, how much of its behavior can emerge on its own? Building a partial digital copy turns that question into an experiment: which parts of the wiring and chemistry are enough to explain behavior, and what are we still missing? We start with flies, then aim to extend this approach to larger mammals as more complete brain maps become available.”
